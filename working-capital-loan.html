Understand Working Capital

Finance the gap between business expenses and incoming cash.

A business can have regular sales and still face temporary cash-flow pressure. Working capital finance is designed around these operational funding requirements.

Depending on the business and lender, the available structure may include a working capital term loan, cash credit or overdraft, invoice-based financing, or other cash-flow-linked facilities.

Possible Structures

Working capital is not a single loan product

Different businesses may qualify for different structures. The appropriate route depends on turnover, banking, cash flow, business vintage, credit profile and lender policy.

TERM FINANCE

Working Capital Term Loan

A structured loan where repayment is generally made through scheduled instalments over an agreed tenure.

LIMIT FACILITY

Cash Credit / OD

A sanctioned working capital limit that may provide flexibility subject to lender terms, drawing power and security requirements.

RECEIVABLES

Invoice Financing

Certain products can provide financing against eligible invoices or receivables, subject to the applicable platform and lender.

CASH FLOW

Cash-Flow Based Finance

Some digital or specialised products assess business cash flows and transaction information for financing decisions.

Who may be considered?

Eligibility is lender-specific. A business with established operations, regular banking activity and demonstrable cash flows may be considered for relevant working capital products.

✓ Established business operations may be preferred for many products.
✓ Business turnover and banking activity can influence eligibility.
✓ Credit history and existing obligations may be assessed.
✓ GST, ITR and financial records may be required depending on product.
✓ Security or collateral requirements can vary by lender and facility.
Eligibility Factors

What lenders generally evaluate

Business Vintage How long the business has been operating and its continuity.
Turnover Revenue level and consistency of business transactions.
Banking Business banking activity, cash flows and transaction behaviour.
Credit Profile Credit history, existing obligations and repayment behaviour.
Financials ITR, GST and financial statements where applicable.
Security Collateral, guarantee or other security depending on the facility.
Documentation

Keep your business records ready

Exact documents vary according to applicant type, lender and financing product. The following are common examples.

Business & KYC

  • PAN and identity/address proof as applicable
  • Business constitution / registration documents
  • Business address proof
  • Udyam registration, where applicable
  • GST registration/details, where applicable

Financial Information

  • Bank statements for the required period
  • ITR and financial statements where applicable
  • GST returns / business transaction information
  • Existing loan or liability details
  • Additional documents requested by the lender
Important: Do not submit sensitive documents through an unsecured public form. Documents should be shared only through the secure channel/process specified by the relevant lender or authorised service provider.

Before Accepting Finance

Compare the complete cost, not just the interest rate.

When a lender provides an offer, review the applicable Key Facts Statement (KFS), Annual Percentage Rate (APR), processing fees, repayment schedule and other charges where applicable.

Also understand whether the facility requires collateral, guarantee, insurance or other conditions before accepting it.
